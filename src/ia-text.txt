 SourcingRecord · Strategy and structure · 14 October 2026 A reviewable record for every sourcing decision Research, website information architecture, visual direction, and a complete planning specification for a supervised tail-spend procurement concept. Recommended positioning:  SourcingRecord is a proposed RFQ comparison and review workspace for midmarket indirect procurement teams. Start with one repeatable category, show the evidence behind every comparison, and keep supplier engagement inside explicit consent and human authority. Scope: research and IA only. This deliverable specifies the future website and isolated synthetic demo; it does not implement either. The supplied company brief takes priority over the master guide. SourcingRecord and sourcingrecord.com replace Procurement Harbor and its old proposed domain. Research combined the configured Perplexity high research preset with direct first-party browser inspection. Where research retrieval could not access SourcingRecord, the browser resolved the domain to a parking page. No buyer interviews or independent performance validation were conducted. The hypothesis worth putting in front of buyers “Can a reviewable, category-specific comparison reveal a missed alternative without adding more review work or harming a supplier relationship?”  This is a testable entry point. There is no evidence yet that SourcingRecord outperforms incumbent tools, category managers, sourcing suites, or email RFQs. Buyer and champion:  indirect procurement head; category manager or named sourcing owner runs the test. Finance reviews baseline treatment and budget; IT/security and counsel review data and authority when needed. First job:  inspect anonymized closed RFQs for pack-size, specification, lead-time, and term differences. Quote comparability is the recommended first module to test, while preserving all five offers as independent options. First live boundary:  a single repeatable category; explicit supplier consent; manager-authorized non-price terms; no autonomous contact. Safety specifications remain fixed and require qualified human review. Unknowns:  repeat frequency, enough willing suppliers, cost of review, category budget, data access, baseline comparability, willingness to pay, and incremental value over the current process. Expansion:  additional categories and supplier management only after verified net value and supplier experience survive control testing. Treat “supplier-friendly,” “defensible savings,” and “less work” as design goals until evidence exists. The website can explain the evaluation method; it cannot announce those outcomes as achieved. 
 02 · Art direction References to learn from selectively The recommended look is an editorial procurement workbench: warm light surfaces, deep ink, restrained green, precise tables, and document annotations. The signature visual is a supplier comparison linked to its source and approval record. The premium effect should come from composition, legibility, and detail. Three distinct territories RECOMMENDED 1. Editorial record Warm paper, forest ink, marginal notes, large serif marketing headings, crisp sans-serif UI. Asymmetric hero with an open decision record; ruled rows for the offers. Signature: an evidence reference travels from quote to comparison to review. Best fit for SourcingRecord's name and deliberate decision process. 2. Precision workbench White and graphite, restrained rust accent, sans-serif headings, high-density product composition. Full-width comparison table anchors the opening. Signature: raw units visibly resolve into comparable rows. More operational and technical; requires careful mobile simplification. 3. Commercial journal Ivory and muted burgundy, editorial columns, numbered chapters, document crops and annotated redlines. Starts with a sourcing question and unfolds one case. Signature: a decision dossier that accumulates evidence. More distinctive and narrative; keep the product and CTA immediately visible. Brand foundation before high fidelity Develop an original SourcingRecord wordmark and a compact record-tab mark, with monochrome and reverse versions. Derive the favicon from the mark and check it at 16 and 32 pixels. Initial recommended palette: paper  #F6F5EF , ink  #242C29 , forest  #314B3E , muted brass  #927443 . These are proposed directions, not final accessible tokens. Evaluate a text serif for headlines, a neutral sans-serif for body/UI, and tabular figures for quantities; confirm font licensing before selection. Use a 12-column desktop grid, 64–72px maximum hero type, 16–18px body text, 13–14px minimum dense UI text, consistent 4/8px spacing increments, restrained 6–10px corner radii, and borders before shadows. Distinguish pending, blocked, approved, and simulated-complete with text and icons as well as color. Use short transitions to reveal a source, normalize a row, or show a redline. Avoid scroll locking, floating decorative cards, glowing orbs, stock handshake imagery, robot art, automatic savings counters, and five interchangeable icon cards. Reduced-motion mode exposes the same complete information immediately.

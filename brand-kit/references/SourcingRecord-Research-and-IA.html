SourcingRecord · Strategy and structure · 14 October 2026

A reviewable record for every sourcing decision

Research, website information architecture, visual direction, and a complete planning specification for a supervised tail-spend procurement concept.

Recommended positioning: SourcingRecord is a proposed RFQ comparison and review workspace for midmarket indirect procurement teams. Start with one repeatable category, show the evidence behind every comparison, and keep supplier engagement inside explicit consent and human authority.

Scope: research and IA only. This deliverable specifies the future website and isolated synthetic demo; it does not implement either. The supplied company brief takes priority over the master guide. SourcingRecord and sourcingrecord.com replace Procurement Harbor and its old proposed domain.

The hypothesis worth putting in front of buyers

“Can a reviewable, category-specific comparison reveal a missed alternative without adding more review work or harming a supplier relationship?” This is a testable entry point. There is no evidence yet that SourcingRecord outperforms incumbent tools, category managers, sourcing suites, or email RFQs.

  • Buyer and champion: indirect procurement head; category manager or named sourcing owner runs the test. Finance reviews baseline treatment and budget; IT/security and counsel review data and authority when needed.
  • First job: inspect anonymized closed RFQs for pack-size, specification, lead-time, and term differences. Quote comparability is the recommended first module to test, while preserving all five offers as independent options.
  • First live boundary: a single repeatable category; explicit supplier consent; manager-authorized non-price terms; no autonomous contact. Safety specifications remain fixed and require qualified human review.
  • Unknowns: repeat frequency, enough willing suppliers, cost of review, category budget, data access, baseline comparability, willingness to pay, and incremental value over the current process.
  • Expansion: additional categories and supplier management only after verified net value and supplier experience survive control testing.
Treat “supplier-friendly,” “defensible savings,” and “less work” as design goals until evidence exists. The website can explain the evaluation method; it cannot announce those outcomes as achieved.

Three distinct territories

RECOMMENDED

1. Editorial record

Warm paper, forest ink, marginal notes, large serif marketing headings, crisp sans-serif UI. Asymmetric hero with an open decision record; ruled rows for the offers.

Signature: an evidence reference travels from quote to comparison to review. Best fit for SourcingRecord's name and deliberate decision process.

2. Precision workbench

White and graphite, restrained rust accent, sans-serif headings, high-density product composition. Full-width comparison table anchors the opening.

Signature: raw units visibly resolve into comparable rows. More operational and technical; requires careful mobile simplification.

3. Commercial journal

Ivory and muted burgundy, editorial columns, numbered chapters, document crops and annotated redlines. Starts with a sourcing question and unfolds one case.

Signature: a decision dossier that accumulates evidence. More distinctive and narrative; keep the product and CTA immediately visible.

Brand foundation before high fidelity

Develop an original SourcingRecord wordmark and a compact record-tab mark, with monochrome and reverse versions. Derive the favicon from the mark and check it at 16 and 32 pixels. Initial recommended palette: paper #F6F5EF, ink #242C29, forest #314B3E, muted brass #927443. These are proposed directions, not final accessible tokens. Evaluate a text serif for headlines, a neutral sans-serif for body/UI, and tabular figures for quantities; confirm font licensing before selection.

Use a 12-column desktop grid, 64–72px maximum hero type, 16–18px body text, 13–14px minimum dense UI text, consistent 4/8px spacing increments, restrained 6–10px corner radii, and borders before shadows. Distinguish pending, blocked, approved, and simulated-complete with text and icons as well as color.

Use short transitions to reveal a source, normalize a row, or show a redline. Avoid scroll locking, floating decorative cards, glowing orbs, stock handshake imagery, robot art, automatic savings counters, and five interchangeable icon cards. Reduced-motion mode exposes the same complete information immediately.

Deliberate adaptations of the master guide

“How it works” carries the shared platform explanation; the homepage and use cases carry the problem/solution narrative. A baseline-method page carries the evaluation methodology instead of fabricated results. There is no customer page, testimonial wall, live integration directory, pricing checkout, or invented team page. Product pages remain distinct. The sign-in route is an honest access state until real authentication exists. This preserves information coverage without padding the site with repetitive or misleading pages.

Footer: five products; how it works; use cases; demo; controls; supplier experience; pilot/pricing; three guides; about; contact; privacy; terms; pilot access. Social links appear only once their real destinations are verified. Use a copyright line without inventing a legal company suffix.

Three buyer journeys

  1. Procurement buyer: category problem → comparison module → source evidence → demo exception → pilot hypothesis → qualified discussion.
  2. Supplier or skeptical reviewer: controls → supplier experience → consent/transcript/opt-out → binding authority → contact.
  3. Consultant or P2P partner: use cases → sample-preparation guide → one-category diagnostic → named owner and permissioned pilot discussion.

Supplier transcript example

12 October 2026, 09:00 UTC — demo invitation: “Forgefield is reviewing RF-202. This optional demonstration covers delivery timing only. Any draft needs human approval. You may decline or stop participating.”

09:05 — fictional Juniper representative: “I agree to discuss delivery timing for this example. Our quoted total remains $1,020.”

14 October, 10:03 — nonbinding draft: “Proposed delivery is 30 days after order instead of 15, within the approved range. All other terms remain unchanged. This draft has not been sent or accepted.”

Alternative branch: “I do not wish to participate.” Result: “Supplier declined · Further negotiation blocked.” Provide “Contact a human” as a documented future support path, not a fake inbox.

A failed recommendation must be visible

An initial suggestion to choose Juniper is held because the prior purchase has a 15-day lead time. The reviewer cannot silently declare a 30-day supply equivalent. A baseline reviewer must document a defensible adjustment or confirm why the difference has no economic effect for this order; otherwise keep savings unverified. This demonstrates the intended method more honestly than a success-only tour.

Illustrative arithmetic if comparability is explicitly approved with zero delivery adjustment: $1,140 − $1,020 = $120 gross purchase-cost difference; 0.5 review hours × $60/hour = $30; $90 before pilot-fee allocation and other incremental costs. Attributing the entire hypothetical $2,500 pilot fee to this single order yields −$2,410. This is not a commercially viable one-order result and must not become a public ROI claim. Repeated purchase frequency, allocation and actual net value require buyer evidence.

08 · Acquisition and decision gates

Use the website to test a bounded purchase decision

Sequence: target-buyer conversation → permissioned anonymized closed-RFQ diagnostic → compare missed alternatives against buyer-reviewed ground truth → select one module/category → agree scope, owner, envelope, supplier consent and data handling → seek a paid pilot → supervised live RFQ → evaluate net value and supplier experience.

Proposed LinkedIn and paid-test copy

Consultant/category-manager outreach draft: “When your team compares repeat-purchase RFQs, do pack sizes or lead times make the lowest quote hard to judge? We’re researching SourcingRecord, a supervised comparison workflow. The fictional demo shows three quotes, the source assumptions and a human approval checkpoint. Would a closed-RFQ diagnostic be useful for one repeatable category? No live supplier contact is part of the demo.”

Paid-test draft: “Three quotes. Different pack sizes. One reviewable comparison. Explore the SourcingRecord DEMO for repeat tail-spend RFQs. Synthetic data; research-stage concept. Category pilot pricing is hypothetical.” CTA: “Explore demo.” On the destination, show the actual hypothesis price and pilot gates clearly.

Distribution: procurement consultancies, category-manager communities, portfolio-company shared-services teams, and P2P implementation firms. These are acquisition hypotheses, not existing partnerships. Start with one category and low-risk non-price terms; do not frame suppliers as opponents.

Proposed test plan: two messages to the same relevant professional audience—unit comparability versus lead-time exceptions. Set a user-approved total cap and stop date before running any spend; no ads or outreach are sent in this phase. No sensitive-record retargeting. Compare buyer-qualified calls and paid-pilot commitments by source, with clicks and CTR only diagnostic.
